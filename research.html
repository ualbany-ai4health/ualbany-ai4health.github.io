How we work

How we keep a model honest.

RecordThe trapSplitTuneTest
Step 1 · Record

Every sample keeps its patient's ID.

In this sketch, forty people with six recordings each. In a real study it is thousands of 30-second sleep epochs or MRI slices per person, and half the cohort may share a diagnosis. No sample ever loses track of whose body it came from.

Step 2 · The trap

Shuffling samples leaks people into the test set.

Split the samples at random and most patients end up on both sides. A model can then score well by recognising a person's signal or anatomy rather than the disease, and the number collapses on the next hospital's patients. It is a common way reported accuracy gets inflated.

Step 3 · Split

Split by person, balanced by diagnosis.

Whole patients go to the test group, with the same share of patients and controls as the full cohort. Every sample of a test patient is sealed away before any modelling starts.

Step 4 · Tune

Every choice is made inside training.

Architecture, features and thresholds are chosen by cross-validation over the training patients only: in each round one fold of patients checks a model trained on the others. Folds, too, are split by person.

Step 5 · Test

The score comes from strangers.

The final model is scored once, on the sealed patients. With small cohorts we rotate the sealed group as well, which is nested cross-validation, and report the spread across rounds, not only the best one.
